I've spent a decade and a half moving through IT — from fixing individual devices, to administrating systems for a hundred-plus person company, to supporting law firms that can't afford a single dropped ball.

Somewhere in the mix, the same instinct kept showing up: when something worked, I wanted to know if I could teach someone else to run it too. That instinct built training programs at the Better Business Bureau, took me to Chicago and Kenya to teach repair skills for iFixit, and now shapes how I write documentation, structure automation, and think about the next thing I want to build — whether it's a script or a story.

That same itch for pulling things apart shows up off the clock, too — most recently as a Quest headset that's been disassembled more than it's actually been used to play anything, just to see how the tracking and passthrough cameras work.
